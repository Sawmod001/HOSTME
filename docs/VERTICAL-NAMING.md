# HostMe — Vertical Naming Strategy

## The Problem

HostMe currently uses two vertical names for its listing categories:

| Current Name | What It Covers |
|---|---|
| `venue` | Event spaces, lounges, bars, party halls, karaoke spots |
| `housing` | Short-lets, apartments, short-term rentals |

Both names are weak. "Venue" is generic and used by every competitor. "Housing" is wrong — it sounds like government policy or long-term residential, not a consumer booking platform.

The goal is to find names that are:
- **Immediately understood** by Nigerian users
- **Memorable** and easy to spell
- **Distinctive** from competitors
- **Premium** feeling, not cheap
- **Scalable** as the platform grows

## Ranking: Event/Party Vertical Names

### Rank 1: **Spot**

**Why it works:**
- Universally used by young Nigerians: "Where the spot dey tonight?"
- Short, punchy, one syllable — extremely memorable
- Works in both English and Pidgin
- Feels casual, youthful, accessible — perfect for HostMe's target demographic
- No major Nigerian platform uses "Spot" as their primary category label
- Pairing: "Find your **spot**" / "Browse **spots**"

**Risk:** Slightly informal; may not convey corporate events/conferences.

### Rank 2: **Venue**

**Why it works:**
- Most universally understood word for this concept
- Used by Whispers, Venues.ng, iSabiVenues, Pukena, Proppizo
- Works across all event types: weddings, corporate, birthday parties
- Premium and professional enough for corporate use

**Risk:** Very common; won't differentiate HostMe from competitors.

### Rank 3: **Space**

**Why it works:**
- Used globally by Peerspace, Splacer, FindASpace
- Conveys creativity, flexibility, modernity
- More versatile than "venue" — implies the space can be transformed
- Feels premium and tech-forward

**Risk:** Slightly less intuitive than "venue" for Nigerian users.

### Rank 4: **Gather**

**Why it works:**
- Action-oriented — focuses on what people DO (gather)
- Warm, communal, human — implies bringing people together
- Modern, brandable, memorable

**Risk:** Not a noun in common use for a place; requires education.

### Rank 5: **Owanbe**

**Why it works:**
- Deeply cultural Nigerian term
- Instantly recognizable to Yoruba speakers
- Evokes energy, celebration, opulence
- Unique and brandable

**Risk:** Yoruba-origin; may not resonate equally across all regions.

## Ranking: Short-Term Rental Vertical Names

### Rank 1: **Shortlet**

**Why it works:**
- THE definitive Nigerian term for short-term rental
- Platforms literally named after it: Shortlet.ng, ShortRent.app
- Immediately understood by every Nigerian user
- Pairing: "Find a **shortlet**" / "Browse **shortlets**"

**Risk:** Very commonly used, so HostMe won't own the term. But as a category label, it is the clearest choice.

**Critical note:** Your current term "housing" actively hurts the product. It sounds like government policy or long-term residential. Users searching for this are looking for "shortlets" — that is the word in their heads.

### Rank 2: **Stays**

**Why it works:**
- Used by Airbnb, Booking.com, and modern travel platforms
- Short, clean, universal
- Implies temporary accommodation without specificity
- Feels modern and premium

**Risk:** Less specifically Nigerian; may feel generic.

### Rank 3: **Short-let**

**Why it works:**
- Same as "Shortlet" but with hyphen — matches formal/legal spelling
- Slightly more formal
- Used by Lokate and legal texts

**Risk:** Hyphens can feel clunky in app UI.

### Rank 4: **Nest**

**Why it works:**
- Warm, cozy, safe — implies home-away-from-home
- Modern and brandable
- Works well in Nigerian context

**Risk:** Less direct about temporary nature; requires brand education.

### Rank 5: **Pad**

**Why it works:**
- Casual, youthful — "my pad for the weekend"
- Short, memorable, one syllable
- Nigerian youth use "pad" informally for apartment

**Risk:** Too casual; may feel cheap; implies permanence.

---

## Recommended Pairings

### Option A (Recommended): "Spots" + "Shortlets"

- "Browse **Spots**" (event/party venues)
- "Browse **Shortlets**" (temporary stays)

**Why:** Authentically Nigerian, modern, immediately clear. "Spot" differentiates from competitors using "Venue." "Shortlet" gives users exactly the word they search for.

### Option B: "Venues" + "Shortlets"

- "Browse **Venues**" (event/party spaces)
- "Browse **Shortlets**" (temporary stays)

**Why:** Safest, most conventional choice. Users instantly know what they're looking at. However, doesn't differentiate.

### Option C: "Spaces" + "Stays"

- "Find a **Space**" (event/party venues)
- "Find a **Stay**" (temporary stays)

**Why:** Most globally modern and tech-forward. Echoes Peerspace/Splacer for venues and Airbnb for accommodation. Less specifically Nigerian but more internationally scalable.

## Sub-Vertical Naming

### Current Sub-Verticals (under venue)

| Current Key | Current Label | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| `birthday` | Birthday | Fine — universal term |
| `exclusive_space` | Exclusive Space | Confusing — overlaps with "exclusive" booking type |
| `karaoke` | Karaoke | Fine — distinct activity |
| `group_night` | Group Night | Non-standard — not used by any other platform |

### Recommended Changes

| Current | Recommended | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `exclusive_space` | `private_event` | Avoids confusion with booking type; matches industry term |
| `group_night` | `hangout` | More natural Nigerian usage; "Group Night" is a HostMe invention |
| `birthday` | `birthday` (no change) | Universal term |
| `karaoke` | `karaoke` (no change) | Distinct activity type |

## Booking Type Naming

### Current Names

| Current | What It Means |
|---|---|
| `capacity` | Pay-per-seat in a shared experience |
| `exclusive` | Private hire of the full space |

### Recommended Changes

| Current | Recommended Label | Keep Enum Value? |
|---|---|---|
| `capacity` | **Per-Seat** | Yes (`capacity`) |
| `exclusive` | **Private Hire** | Yes (`exclusive`) |

**Why:**
- "Capacity" is a backend measurement term. Users don't think "I'm making a capacity booking."
- "Per-Seat" immediately communicates: you pay for one spot, not the whole space.
- "Private Hire" is the global standard (Tagvenue, UK venues). "Exclusive" alone is ambiguous — exclusive to whom?

## Implementation Note

The safest approach is to change **display labels first** (keeping enum values in the database), then plan a migration for enum key renames later. This avoids breaking the existing database while immediately improving the user experience.

For the vertical enum values (`venue`, `housing`), a database migration would be needed:
```sql
UPDATE listings SET vertical = 'spots' WHERE vertical = 'venue';
UPDATE listings SET vertical = 'shortlets' WHERE vertical = 'housing';
ALTER TABLE listings DROP CONSTRAINT listings_vertical_check;
ALTER TABLE listings ADD CONSTRAINT listings_vertical_check CHECK (vertical IN ('spots', 'shortlets'));
```
